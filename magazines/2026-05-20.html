01 · Research Insights of the Day
Systematic Partisan Content Skews in TikTok During the 2024 US Elections
Homa Hosseinmardi, Amir Ghasemian, Aaron Clauset et al. — Nature, May 2026
Across 323 audit experiments using controlled sock-puppet accounts in New York, Texas, and Georgia, researchers collected more than 280,000 recommendations over 27 weeks during the 2024 presidential campaign. The findings are striking: Republican-seeded accounts received roughly 11.5 percent more co-partisan content than their Democratic counterparts, while Democrat-seeded accounts were exposed to about 7.5 percent more cross-partisan material. In other words, TikTok's For You feed systematically steered all users toward right-leaning political content, regardless of their initial leanings. Democrat-leaning accounts were pushed more cross-partisan content on immigration and crime, while Republican-leaning accounts saw more content on abortion.
Practitioner implication: Campaigns relying on TikTok's organic reach should understand that the platform's recommendation engine may amplify conservative messaging asymmetrically. Democratic strategists should factor algorithmic headwinds into paid distribution budgets and consider platform-specific content strategies that account for cross-partisan exposure.
Source: Nature (May 2026) · See also: Nature News

04 · US Midterms · Loyalty Purge

Trump Endorses Paxton Over Cornyn: The Loyalty Purge Reaches Texas

President Trump delivered an 11th-hour endorsement of Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton in the state's Republican Senate runoff against three-term incumbent John Cornyn, just one week before the May 26 vote. The endorsement could end the career of the Senate's longest-serving Republican, and it signals that no incumbent — however senior — is safe from Trump's loyalty test. Neither candidate cleared 50 percent in March's first round, setting up a bruising runoff that has already drawn tens of millions in spending.

The stakes extend beyond Texas. Democrats see the seat as a potential stretch pickup in November, with nominee James Talarico waiting in the wings. A weakened or polarizing Republican nominee could make this traditionally safe seat competitive for the first time in a generation. Trump framed the choice in personal terms, praising Paxton as a fighter and faulting Cornyn for insufficient loyalty during his political trials.

Sources: NPR, Texas Tribune, Axios

Axios Harris Poll 100: AI Trust Splits Along Party Lines

The 2026 Axios Harris Poll 100, released May 19, reveals a dramatic partisan fracture in how Americans perceive the AI industry. Republicans now rate OpenAI 12 points higher than Democrats do — a gap that barely existed two years ago. TikTok, Nvidia, Meta, and X show similarly widening divides. Democrats have grown more skeptical of AI technology and the industry behind it, while Republicans are significantly more likely to trust most AI companies.

One notable exception stands out: Anthropic, maker of Claude, ranks No. 15 overall among America's most visible brands with only a 1-point partisan gap — making it the rare AI company trusted equally across the aisle. Generationally, 42 percent of Gen Z respondents believe AI will harm their job opportunities and wages, the highest of any age group. The poll underscores a new reality: AI is no longer a neutral technology issue — it is a partisan identity marker.

Sources: Axios, The Harris Poll
06 · GovTech · AI in Public Services

Code for America × Anthropic: AI Meets the Food-Stamp Caseworker

Code for America and Anthropic announced a partnership at the 2026 Code for America Summit in Chicago to deploy Claude-based tools that help government caseworkers administer public benefits more efficiently. The pilot begins with SNAP — the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program — through a Policy Navigator that lets caseworkers type plain-language questions about eligibility rules and receive cited, up-to-date answers with suggested next steps.

The collaboration goes beyond a single chatbot. The organizations plan a full suite of tools for reviewing eligibility documents, drafting communications to benefit recipients, and answering complex policy questions that currently require caseworkers to search through hundreds of pages of federal and state regulation. The long-term vision is a set of reusable, open tools that can be adapted across states and counties — a rare example of AI deployment in government that prioritizes the front-line worker rather than the policy-maker.

07 · Global Elections · Ongoing

Colombia: Cepeda Holds the Lead but Runoff Math Favors the Right

Ongoing — New development: right-wing vote consolidation shifts second-round projections

With 11 days until Colombia's first round on May 31, the picture is clarifying: nobody will win outright. Pacto Histórico senator Iván Cepeda leads first-round polling at 35 to 44 percent depending on the survey, well short of the 50-percent threshold for an outright victory. Centro Democrático senator Paloma Valencia has consolidated the right-wing vote and surged to 25 percent in the latest Fundación Génesis Crea survey, while lawyer Abelardo de la Espriella holds around 21 percent.

The critical twist is in the second-round math. While Cepeda dominates the first round, multiple polls now project Valencia beating him 49.1 percent to 40.6 percent in a June 21 runoff, as De la Espriella's voters break overwhelmingly to the right. This dynamic mirrors Colombia's 2022 pattern, where left-wing first-round leaders have historically struggled in head-to-head matchups when the right coalesces.

The race carries high stakes for U.S.–Latin American relations. A Cepeda presidency would continue Petro’s leftist project, while a Valencia win would mark a sharp rightward correction. International observers are watching whether security concerns and the economy — not ideology — ultimately decide the electorate.

Sources: AS/COA Poll Tracker, ColombiaOne, Rio Times
08 · Social Media Trends · Political Psychology

The Parasocial Ballot: How Social Media Intimacy Shapes Votes

A growing body of research is revealing that the one-sided sense of intimacy users develop with politicians on social media — known as parasocial relationships — may be a more powerful predictor of voting intention than traditional party loyalty or policy alignment. A 2025 study of the Indonesian presidential election found that when users felt a sense of homophily (similarity) and personal connection with a political figure online, the emotion of pleasure became the strongest mediator linking those experiences to both electronic word-of-mouth and actual voting intention.

The implications for the 2026 midterms are direct. Candidates who master the grammar of social media intimacy — behind-the-scenes content, personal stories, direct replies — may be building electoral advantages that traditional polling misses entirely. As one researcher put it, the feeling that a politician is talking directly to you creates a bond that functions like personal endorsement. In a cycle where both parties are fighting for disengaged voters, the parasocial advantage may matter more than the ground game.

ScienceDirect (2025), Communication Research

The AI Fundraising Machine Is Already Running

More than half a billion dollars has been raised through AI-powered fundraising methods in the 2026 midterm cycle, according to aggregated reporting from campaign finance trackers and political technology firms. The tools range from AI-optimized email subject lines and donation-page personalization to predictive models that identify likely small donors before they've ever given.

The National Republican Senatorial Committee has been a particularly aggressive adopter, using AI to create ad variants — including video-game-themed spots targeting Democratic Senate candidates — and to identify new small-dollar donors at scale. On the Democratic side, adoption has been slower but not absent: progressive organizations are using AI primarily for data analysis and volunteer coordination rather than direct donor solicitation. The $500 million figure represents a step change from 2024, when AI-driven fundraising was still largely experimental.

11 · AI in Campaigns · Ongoing

GOP Simulates Voters With AI Agents While Democrats Ban ChatGPT

Ongoing: The partisan AI divide in campaigns, a recurring theme this cycle, deepened further this week. An Axios investigation found that 64 percent of Republican consultants now use AI daily in their work, compared to 49 percent of Democrats — a gap driven as much by institutional culture as by ideology. The Republican National Committee's embrace is systemic: tools like Aaru, which replicates pools of voters as AI agents that can be polled on their views, are being deployed to simulate voter reactions to messaging before ads ever air.

The Democratic side tells a more complicated story. The DNC has barred staffers from using ChatGPT and Claude, though it permits Gemini for coding and data analysis — a distinction that reflects regulatory anxiety more than technological assessment. Progressive campaigns, meanwhile, are using AI primarily behind the scenes for data crunching and volunteer logistics, wary of the optics of AI-generated voter contact in a cycle where their base is deeply skeptical of the technology. The result is an emerging asymmetry that could shape which party communicates more effectively in the final stretch.

Axios, TIME

Georgia’s Governor Race: Third Time Democrats Nominate a Black Woman

Former Atlanta Mayor Keisha Lance Bottoms cleared 50 percent on May 19 to win the Democratic gubernatorial nomination outright, defeating a field that included former DeKalb County CEO Michael Thurmond, former state Sen. Bottoms enters the general election with significant name recognition from her tenure as Atlanta mayor and her role in the Biden administration. Former President Biden endorsed her in the primary's final stretch. But Democrats have not won the Georgia governor's mansion in 24 years, and the Republican runoff between Lt. Gov. Burt Jones and billionaire Rick Jackson will determine which brand of conservatism she faces in November. The race will test whether Georgia's shifting demographics can finally deliver a statewide win for Democrats in an off-year election.

13 · Split Graph
Two Perspectives on One Issue
The AI Campaign Divide: Innovation or Recklessness?

The Caution Case

Progressive strategists and civil-liberties advocates argue that rushing AI into campaign operations risks voter privacy, entrenches manipulation, and widens the digital divide. The DNC's restrictions on ChatGPT and Claude reflect a principle: the potential for AI-generated misinformation and micro-targeted persuasion at scale outweighs the efficiency gains. When campaigns can simulate entire electorates as AI agents, the line between persuasion and manipulation becomes dangerously thin.

Democrats worry that normalizing AI in elections erodes public trust at a moment when trust is already at historic lows. Better to win slowly and transparently than to win fast and lose legitimacy.

Sources: Axios (April 14, 2026), The American Prospect (October 2025)

The Innovation Case

Republican operatives and campaign-tech firms counter that AI is simply the latest tool in a long lineage of political technology — from direct mail to micro-targeting — and that refusing to use it is unilateral disarmament. With 64 percent of GOP consultants using AI daily, the party argues it is meeting voters where they are, with more personalized, responsive messaging. Tools like Aaru and EyesOver, they say, make campaigns more responsive to actual voter sentiment rather than lagging polls.

For the innovation camp, the risk is not in using AI but in falling behind. In a cycle where every dollar and minute counts, the party that communicates faster and more precisely will have the edge.

Sources: Axios (April 14, 2026), CNBC (May 19, 2026), Campaign Nucleus

14 · Political Tech Application
$ eyesover --sentiment-scan

EyesOver is a real-time public-sentiment analysis platform that scans millions of social media posts per day to identify how opinions, emotions, and narratives are shifting as events unfold. Integrated with Campaign Nucleus — the Republican Party's primary campaign management platform — it connects sentiment intelligence directly to campaign execution, turning insight into ad buys, messaging pivots, and rapid response within a single workflow.

Multiple Republican campaigns in the 2026 midterm cycle are using EyesOver to pre-test messaging before committing to ad spending. One GOP strategist described it as being able to see the battlefield in real time rather than waiting for weekly polling. The National Republican Senatorial Committee has used the platform to identify emerging micro-narratives — such as localized economic anxiety or trending policy frustrations — and deploy targeted digital ads within hours of detection.

The tool's claimed accuracy in predicting trend direction has made it a fixture in Republican campaign war rooms, and its integration with Campaign Nucleus means that the gap between sensing a shift and acting on it is measured in hours, not days.

> takeaway: EyesOver represents the maturation of real-time sentiment analysis from a research curiosity into an operational campaign tool — and the party that adopted it first is building a structural advantage in responsiveness.
Sources: Campaign Nucleus, Axios
