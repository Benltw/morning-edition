Political Psychology

Both AI-Generated and Human Influencers Can Correct Misinformation

Computers in Human Behavior · 2025

Researchers tested whether AI-generated social media influencers could correct political misinformation as effectively as human influencers. The experiment revealed a striking asymmetry: both human and AI influencers successfully corrected misbeliefs about non-polarized topics such as mental health. But on polarized political issues — including climate change — only the AI-generated influencers managed to shift participants' views toward factual accuracy. The authors theorize that AI personas may bypass the partisan identity cues that cause people to reject corrections from human sources perceived as politically motivated. The finding complicates the prevailing narrative that synthetic media is purely a threat to democratic discourse; under certain conditions, it may also serve as a corrective tool.

Practitioner implication: Campaigns and civic organizations designing counter-misinformation strategies may find that AI-generated corrective content outperforms human messengers on precisely the issues where correction is hardest — partisan flashpoints.

Source: Computers in Human Behavior, ScienceDirect, 2025. doi:10.1016/S0747-5632(25)00292-4

Reform UK Sweeps Hundreds of Council Seats as Labour Hemorrhages

Ongoing: The UK's local election story has shifted from projection to reality. Results pouring in on Friday morning show Reform UK gaining at least 150 council seats, taking territory from both Labour and the Conservatives. In Hartlepool, Reform won all twelve seats up for election — seven from Labour, four from the Conservatives, and one from an independent. In Tamworth, all nine contested seats flipped to Reform. Labour has lost control of councils in Southampton and Tameside, and Prime Minister Keir Starmer's party is bracing for further losses as London results arrive later Friday. Elections expert Sir John Curtice described Reform as being "in poll position," and early national projections suggest the party could expand to over 2,000 councillors if trends hold. The results mark the most significant local election realignment in a generation, confirming that Reform's insurgency has moved from protest movement to governing reality at the council level.

Sources: BBC News, ITV News, LBC, WRAL/AP — May 8, 2026

Tennessee Signs Map Dismantling Memphis's Black-Majority District

Ongoing: Governor Bill Lee signed Tennessee's new congressional map on Wednesday, formally carving up the state's only majority-Black district — the Memphis-based seat held by longtime Democratic Representative Steve Cohen — into three Republican-controlled districts that stretch hundreds of miles into rural Tennessee. The move is part of a broader GOP redistricting strategy driven by the Supreme Court's weakening of the Voting Rights Act. Demonstrators packed the galleries during the special session vote, and a Democratic state Senator stood on her desk holding a banner denouncing the plan as a "Jim Crow" effort. One Tennessee lawmaker has called for Memphis to secede from the state in protest. Republicans have now enacted new maps in at least five states, targeting thirteen House seats currently held by Democrats, as President Trump works to protect a slim majority ahead of November. Democrats have counter-mapped in California, Virginia, and Utah.

Sources: NBC News, CNN, Daily Memphian, Tennessee Lookout — May 7, 2026

Hezbollah Suffers Historic Parliamentary Defeat in Lebanon

Lebanon's May 15 parliamentary election delivered a seismic result: Hezbollah lost not only its majority control of the legislature but all of its non-Shia Muslim allies. While the group managed to preserve its twenty-seven-member Shia bloc, its support appears to be eroding even within that core constituency — all Shia districts recorded lower turnout compared with 2018, suggesting a significant silent majority has turned against the party politically. The Christian landscape shifted dramatically: Samir Geagea's Lebanese Forces party now claims the largest Christian representation with over twenty seats, overtaking the formerly Hezbollah-allied Free Patriotic Movement of Gebran Bassil. In Druze areas, opposition independents broke through with three seats. The result strengthens the hand of Prime Minister Nawaf Salam and President Joseph Aoun, and reshapes the debate over Hezbollah's disarmament and the country's sectarian power balance.

Sources: Atlantic Council, The Washington Institute, The New Arab — May 2026

Colombia's Left Holds Commanding Lead as Valencia Surges Late

With three weeks until Colombia's May 31 presidential election, Pacto Historico senator Ivan Cepeda holds 44.3 percent in the most recent Invamer survey of 3,800 voters — a margin that could deliver a first-round victory if it holds above fifty percent on election day. Attorney Abelardo de la Espriella sits at 21.5 percent, but the fastest-rising candidate is Centro Democratico senator Paloma Valencia, who has nearly doubled her support from 10 percent to 19.8 percent in a single polling cycle. Former Bogota mayor Claudia Lopez has collapsed from 11.7 percent to 3.6 percent. Cepeda has consolidated support across all regions, with particularly strong gains among voters aged 20 to 30. If no candidate clears fifty percent, a runoff between the top two will be held on June 21.

Sources: AS/COA Poll Tracker, Rio Times, The City Paper Bogota — April–May 2026

The Enthusiasm Gap: Democrats Hold a 14-Point Edge Over Republicans

Multiple national polls converge on the same finding: Democratic voters want to vote more than Republicans do

An NPR/PBS News/Marist poll released this week found that 61 percent of Democrats say they are "very enthusiastic" about voting in the midterms — virtually identical to the 60 percent who said the same in October 2018, a cycle that produced a blue wave of 40 House seats. The striking figure is on the other side: Republican enthusiasm has dropped from 65 percent in 2018 to just 53 percent today. ABC News-Washington Post polling puts the enthusiasm advantage at 14 points, the widest since 2006. CNN/SSRS data from early April shows 84 percent of Democrats "very" or "extremely" motivated to vote, compared with 74 percent of Republicans. Talk of a blue wave is accelerating — Time magazine reported this week that a 2006-scale Democratic gain "no longer seems far-fetched." President Trump faces his lowest approval ratings since taking office for his second term, with more than 80 percent of Americans blaming the administration for pain at the gas pump. The generic congressional ballot shows Democrats leading by roughly 10 points. Strategists on both sides increasingly describe the environment as structurally similar to the wave cycles of 2006 and 2018, though redistricting and turnout dynamics could blunt the effect.

Sources: NPR/PBS News/Marist, CNN/SSRS, ABC News-Washington Post, Time — May 2026

AI Deepfake Ads Hit 15+ Midterm Races — Still No Federal Law

Ongoing: At least fifteen campaign advertisements featuring AI-generated content have aired since November, according to NBC News, and the number is rising as the midterm season intensifies. The National Republican Senatorial Committee released an AI-generated video of James Talarico, the Democratic Senate nominee in Texas, reading real tweets on race and transgender rights. In Maine, a Republican ad used a deepfake of Democratic candidate Graham Platner. Twenty-eight states have passed legislation addressing AI in political ads — most requiring disclosure rather than banning the practice outright. But there is still no federal regulation: Senator Amy Klobuchar's REAL Political Advertisements Act, introduced in 2023, never reached a floor vote. The OECD has now documented the 2026 midterms as a case study in AI-generated electoral misinformation. Critics warn that the patchwork of state laws, many still untested in court, leaves the vast majority of voters without meaningful protection.

Sources: NBC News, Bangor Daily News, Detroit News, American Prospect, OECD.AI — 2026

GOP Goes All-In on AI Campaign Tools. Democrats? Not So Much.

A survey of campaign consultants reported by Axios finds that 64 percent of Republican consultants now use AI daily in their work, compared with 49 percent of Democrats. The gap is not just in adoption rates but in ambition: Republican campaigns are using AI to produce entire video advertisements, simulate voter panels, scan millions of social media posts for sentiment trends, and deliver personalized talking points through AI chatbots in private text messages. High-profile Senate campaigns have aired ads that were fully or mostly AI-generated. On the Democratic side, adoption has been slower and more cautious, with many operatives citing ethical concerns and a preference for human-led messaging. The partisan AI divide is emerging as one of the defining tactical stories of the 2026 cycle — a mirror of the broader cultural split over technology's role in public life.

Sources: Axios, Deseret News, American Prospect — April–May 2026

Utah Signs Nine AI Bills in One Session — Most Ambitious State AI Package of the Year

Governor Spencer Cox signed all nine AI-related bills that reached his desk before the legislature adjourned on May 6, making Utah the most active state on AI regulation this session. The package includes mandatory disclosure requirements for AI-generated political advertisements, new consumer protections around AI-driven decision-making, and guidelines for public-sector AI procurement. Utah's approach contrasts sharply with Colorado, which scrapped its landmark AI Act last week and started over, and with Connecticut, which passed a comprehensive but less granular law. The nine-bill approach reflects a modular legislative philosophy: rather than a single omnibus AI law, Utah's legislators crafted targeted measures that can be individually updated as the technology evolves. The package positions Utah alongside the EU's phased approach under the AI Act, where specific obligations attach to specific risk categories.

Sources: Transparency Coalition AI Legislative Update, NCSL — May 2026

TikTok's Political Feeds Are More Polarized Than Any Other Platform

A new study highlighted by Tubefilter this week finds that TikTok's algorithmic feeds produce more politically polarized content streams than any other major social media platform. Close to 40 percent of U.S. adults aged 18 to 29 now get their news regularly from TikTok, and campaigns are responding: Governor Josh Shapiro's reelection team gave credentialed access to influencers with a reserved section near the stage, treating them as equivalent to traditional press. A separate field experiment published in Party Politics by Alsharawy, Anstett, and Landgrave found that consuming political TikTok content increased negative emotional affect among young adults but produced no measurable change in political attitudes — suggesting that the platform generates heat without light. Both parties are pouring resources into influencer partnerships, but the evidence for persuasive impact remains thin.

Sources: Tubefilter, Party Politics (2026), City & State PA — May 2026

The Redistricting Battle: Who Gets to Draw the Lines?

The Case Against Mid-Decade Redistricting

Critics argue that Republican-led efforts to redraw maps outside the normal census cycle are a deliberate strategy to entrench partisan advantage at the expense of minority representation. Tennessee's dismantling of a majority-Black district, they say, echoes Jim Crow-era tactics of diluting Black voting power. The Supreme Court's weakening of the Voting Rights Act has removed the guardrails that once prevented the worst abuses.

Sources: CNN Analysis, Democracy Docket, The Hill — May 2026

The Case for State Authority Over Maps

Defenders maintain that states have constitutional authority to draw their own congressional districts and that periodic redistricting reflects legitimate population shifts and political realignment. They argue that both parties engage in the practice — pointing to Democratic counter-maps in California and Virginia — and that judicial intervention in line-drawing undermines state sovereignty and legislative prerogative.

Sources: Washington Times, Ballotpedia, Cook Political Report — May 2026

Campaign Technology

> Aaru

AI-Powered Synthetic Voter Simulation Platform

WHAT IT DOES: Aaru uses large language models to create synthetic voter panels — AI agents endowed with demographic profiles, behavioral patterns, and cognitive preferences — that can be polled on their political views in as little as 90 seconds. Each simulation typically involves around 5,000 AI respondents at less than one-tenth the cost of traditional survey research.

WHO IS USING IT: Republican campaigns and consultants have adopted Aaru for rapid message testing, ad effectiveness prediction, and district-level voter universe modeling. Aaru raised over $50 million, signaling serious institutional confidence in synthetic polling.

TRACK RECORD: In the 2024 New York Democratic primary, Aaru's synthetic panel predicted the final result within 371 votes of the actual outcome — a level of accuracy that outperformed most traditional polls in that race.

> takeaway: Synthetic polling compresses weeks of fieldwork into seconds and is already shaping GOP ad strategy for November. If accuracy holds at scale, it could fundamentally reshape how campaigns allocate resources.

Sources: Semafor, New Statesman, Axios, LinkedIn/Leonard Murphy — 2024–2026
