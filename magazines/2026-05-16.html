Political Parasocial Relationships on Social Media: The Role of User Experiences and Emotions in Shaping eWOM and Voting Intention

Hidayanto, A.N., Fitriani, H., Khairunnisa, A.A., Aurelya, K., Kusumo, M.D. & Steven (2025)

Researchers at the University of Indonesia examined how voters' interactions with politicians' social media accounts generate one-sided emotional bonds — parasocial relationships — that ultimately drive both word-of-mouth endorsement and voting intention. Drawing on 412 respondents during the 2024 Indonesian presidential election, the team found that "homophily experiences" (perceived similarity with the candidate) and "relationship experiences" (sense of ongoing personal connection) significantly elevated feelings of pleasure, arousal, and dominance. Among these emotional mediators, pleasure proved the single strongest predictor of both sharing the candidate's content and intending to vote for them. The study is among the first to apply the PAD (Pleasure-Arousal-Dominance) emotional model to political social media behavior at scale.

Practitioner implication: Campaigns investing in parasocial touchpoints — live Q&As, behind-the-scenes content, direct reply interactions — may earn more durable voter commitment than those relying on broadcast advertising alone.

Online Information Review / ScienceDirect, published 2025. DOI: 10.1016/S2772-5030(25)00087-8

Deepfakes in Campaigns: Should States Regulate or Protect Expression?

The Case for Regulation: Voter Protection First

Proponents argue that AI-generated political content — fake candidate statements, fabricated endorsements, manipulated video — poses a direct threat to informed consent at the ballot box. Thirty states have acted precisely because the federal vacuum left voters exposed. Disclosure requirements are content-neutral: they do not ban speech but ensure audiences know what they are watching. The California ruling, they contend, was an outlier driven by overbroad drafting, not a fundamental barrier. Without guardrails, the cheapest and most misleading ads win.

Sources: MultiState, Feb 2026; American Prospect, Apr 2026; NCSL

The Case for Caution: Free Speech and Innovation

Opponents warn that deepfake laws risk chilling legitimate political speech — satire, parody, opposition research — under vague definitions of "substantially altered." California's federal court loss demonstrated the constitutional fragility of these statutes. Critics note that disclosure mandates can be weaponized by incumbents against challengers with fewer resources to navigate compliance. They advocate for platform-level transparency tools and voter media literacy over state censorship regimes that will inevitably be litigated to irrelevance.

Sources: CNN, Mar 2026; Reuters; Fakeout.io; Campaign Now
